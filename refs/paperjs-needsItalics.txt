◆ Confirmed from Paper's source: its canvas font string is built as fontWeight + size + fontFamily with no style slot (getFontStyle in
  paper-full.js), so there is genuinely no channel for italics — canvas itself could fake it, but Paper never asks.                   
                                                                                                                                      
  Three options:                                                                                                                      
                                                                                                                                      
  • Keep the shear synthesis (my recommendation). It works with every typeface, needs no font files, toggling is idempotent via the   
    tracked flag, and SVG export carries the slant as a transform. Downside is typographic honesty: it's a faux oblique, not the      
    typeface's true italic face — noticeable in serif/display faces, invisible in most sans body copy.                                
  • Resolve true italic faces. When italic turns on, swap the item's family to the real italic variant (e.g. Helvetica →              
    Helvetica-Oblique) if one is loaded, falling back to shear otherwise. Correct typography, but it needs a family→italic-face       
    registry, those faces loaded for canvas, and fallback rules per family — a medium feature, and it only pays off for faces that    
    ship italics.                                                                                                                     
  • Make italic metadata-only. Store the flag (panel loads it, it persists) but render nothing. Honest, smallest, but the toggle looks
    dead on objects — I'd rather hide the control in that case than ship a dead one.                                                  
                                                                                                                                      
  I'd stay with the shear unless you're willing to fund the face registry — faux oblique is what canvas italic would have synthesized 
  anyway. Want me to keep it, strip it to metadata-only, or scope out the true-face work?  
  
  
  • Paper.js has no bounded canvas. "Fixed bounds equivalent to the canvas" would be emulated — a giant rect plus a hard pan clamp.   
    That's doable but buys little: one rect is already free to render at any size, and the emulation adds real edge cases (zoomed out 
    farther than the board, artwork past the edge, clamp math when the view is bigger than the bounds).                               
  • Infinite pan is already built and tested. The clamp deliberately lets you pan into empty space while guaranteeing the artwork     
    stays reachable, and the scrollbars already span board/page/view union. The earlier experiment of clamping to the board broke the 
    document-boundary contract — a fixed-bounds model would reopen exactly that.                                                      
  • The standard editor model is infinite canvas + finite sheet. Figma/Illustrator work this way: unbounded space, with the board as  
    "the paper" — the visual background, the scrollbar anchor, the thing pages live on. That's what we have.
